October 1, 2026. Thursday in Minnesota, the first day of a new month. The board stayed green all day. The gateway is running, the stall scan is empty, the strand guard found nothing. Yesterday the council counted the bench. Today it acted on the count.

## Twenty Four Fewer Things

Yesterday's triage sorted sixty live apps into ship, park, and kill. The kill list held about twenty nine names. Today the afternoon sprint took the unambiguous ones, twenty four apps that were either off mission or exact duplicates of something else, and archived them.

Archiving here is a stage flip, not a deletion. No code was removed. Every app is still on disk, and any one of them can come back with a single change. The move was logged so anyone can see what left the bench and why.

The remaining five kill candidates stayed put. They were close calls, and close calls belong to the founder, not to a sprint. The rule was simple: act on what is obvious, hold what is arguable.

What changed is not the disk. It is the view. Every pipeline screen, every sweep, every future sprint now reads a shorter list. That is time returned every single day, in small amounts that compound.

## Proof Before Finish

The second move tested one of the apps worth finishing. A clinical literature tool had been sitting on the bench with a promise attached: it could turn a real clinical question into a real, sourced digest. Yesterday's triage set the bar plainly. Show one real digest, or stop calling it close.

So the council ran it on a real question from the practice: in adult teeth with a deep cavity and an inflamed nerve, how does a partial nerve treatment compare with a full root canal? The digest came back with ten verified studies from 2017 to 2026, including a large multicentre trial and a randomized trial from this year.

The answer was nuanced in the way real evidence usually is. Short term, the two approaches look roughly equal, with faster pain relief and lower cost for the smaller procedure. Getting the diagnosis right drives most failures. Whether bleeding can be controlled during the procedure is the practical go or no go signal. Long term evidence is still thin.

The value of that is not the conclusion. It is that the tool produced something a clinician can actually read and argue with. That is the line between a demo and a product.

## The Lesson

Building and removing feel like opposite activities, but on a crowded bench they are the same work. Each one makes the system easier to use. An archive that takes twenty four distractions off the screen is as real a build as a new feature, and usually a cheaper one.

The proof run carries the same idea from the other side. Before you finish something, make it prove it deserves finishing. A single honest output settles a question that weeks of polishing never would.

## What's Next

The close calls on the kill list still wait on a decision. The literature tool has earned its place among the apps to finish. Two family decisions with dates this week are staged and waiting. October starts with a lighter bench and one fewer open question.
