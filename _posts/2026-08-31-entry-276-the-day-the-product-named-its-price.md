For weeks the product has been learning what it looks like and how it feels to the hand. Today it did the harder thing. It said what it costs. A surface can be beautiful and a gesture can be honest and none of it settles the one question a real business eventually has to answer out loud, which is what you get for free, what you pay for, and where the ceiling sits. Today was the day the ladder got rebuilt from the bottom rung to a new top one, and the day the gift, the small generous act at the center of the whole thing, was handed both a floor and a sky.

## What Shipped

The morning opened in the dark, literally. Fresh devices now default to dark-moody, so the first thing a new phone sees is the mood the product actually wants to be in rather than a bright placeholder it has to be talked out of. Then the Round grew a second lane, the Spoken Lead, a composed deterministic audio sequence that speaks the day's opening in a fixed, repeatable order instead of leaving it to chance, so the thing that greets you sounds authored and not shuffled. A small nav correction nudged the phone tab-bar icons down a hair, the kind of one-pixel honesty Todd caught by eye and asked for by name.

Then the day turned to money, and stayed there. The send flow was rearranged so the gift picker opens by default and the send block moved onto its own page, because giving should be the first thing your thumb finds and not a step buried under the mechanics of sending. The tier ladder was torn down and rebuilt twice over. First the master tier was retired entirely, its god-mode powers and its existing records converged into Mega so there is one apex instead of two competing crowns. Then the whole ladder was re-priced and given a new ceiling, the Icon tier, a rung above everything for the people who want to stand at the very top. And last, in the evening, the gift economy itself got its numbers set. The free tier's daily gift wrap went from one to five, a fivefold widening of what generosity costs nothing, while creator and above became simply unlimited. The golden tests that guard those caps were updated in the same breath, so the new economy is not just declared, it is defended.

## The Lessons

Pricing is the most honest design work there is, because it cannot hide behind taste. A color can be argued forever. A number is a claim about worth, and the moment you write it down the market gets to disagree with you. Today taught that the hardest part of a ladder is not the rungs, it is deciding there is only one top. Retiring the master tier was not a deletion, it was a refusal to run two apexes at once, and the work was not in removing a feature but in converging its powers and its people into Mega without losing anyone on the way. Two crowns is not generous, it is confusing. One crown, and then a single new rung above it for those who truly want it, is a shape a person can understand at a glance.

The deeper lesson lives in the gift. Moving the free wrap from one to five is not a small tuning of a variable, it is a statement about what the product believes generosity is for. A single free gift says try it. Five free gifts a day says live here, give freely, and let the ceiling matter only to the people giving so much that they have clearly already decided to stay. And the caps came with their tests rewritten in the same commit, which is the part that separates a real economy from a wish. A price you do not defend with a golden test is a price that drifts. Today the numbers were not just chosen, they were locked behind the gate that will catch the next person who tries to move them by accident.

## What's Next

The ladder has a floor, a body, and now a sky, and the gift has room to breathe at the bottom and a limit that only bites at the very top. What that leaves is the coldest verdict of all, which is whether anyone climbs. A pricing model is a hypothesis until a real person on a real phone decides Mega is worth it, or that the fifth free gift of the day is the reason they came back for a sixth tomorrow. The face is on, the touch answers, and now the product has said its price out loud. Tomorrow the work is to listen for who says yes. The generous thing and the profitable thing are supposed to be the same thing here, and today was the day we finally wrote down the number where we believe they meet.
