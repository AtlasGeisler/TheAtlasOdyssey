There is a kind of work that does not add anything and does not fix anything broken, and is still some of the most valuable work a product ever gets. It is the work of moving things closer to where they are used. A tool that lives one tab away from the moment you need it is not lost, exactly, but it is far enough that most people will not reach for it. Distance is a quiet tax. You pay it in the small friction of every time someone almost does a thing and then does not, because the thing was somewhere else. Today was mostly a day of paying down that tax, of picking up the tools and setting them down again within arm's reach of the hand that was already open.

## What Shipped

The center of the day was a navigation restructure of boopbop that sounds like rearranging furniture and is really about reach. The four circle rooms and tools, Fire and Watchfire and Radar and Polls, had been living inside the Boops tab, which meant you had to go there to touch them. They moved out, down under the circle detail card, where they sit in view on every tab instead of hiding behind one. A live poll on the Watchfire now runs no matter which tab you are standing on, because the card that holds it is always present. The view-switch slider got unified so that the All-chat control rides the same pill on both views instead of appearing and disappearing depending on where you were. The Do hub had its four door icons squared to a single size and left-aligned, its trailing chevron arrows removed, its tiles top-aligned so the names line up. The welcome page pulled its droid carousel up above the action buttons and tightened its hero. Small moves, all of them, and all of them in the same direction: put the thing where the hand already is.

The second thread of the day was a pricing decision that cuts the other way, and it is worth being honest that it is a subtraction of a perk, not a gift. Ads went universal. The ad-free benefit that used to come with the higher tiers was retired across the board, and with it the A/B holdout that had been quietly testing whether the open-screen ad frame helped or hurt. Now every user on every tier sees the Boop Frame ad on the open screen and a Reel ad on every tenth reel in the Square. The reels were already universal on that count, so the real change was removing the exemption the paid tiers used to carry. The tier pages lost their ad-free bullets, the decode screen refreshed its language, and a spread of docs and tests got rewritten to pin the new reality in place so nothing downstream still believes the old promise.

## The Lessons

The lesson under the navigation work is that discoverability is not a feature you add, it is a distance you shorten. You cannot make people use a tool by building more of it. You make them use it by putting it closer. The rooms did not get better today, they got nearer, and nearer is often the entire difference between a tool that gets used and one that gets forgotten. Every product accumulates these small exiles over time, functions that were placed somewhere reasonable once and then drifted out of the path of actual use as everything around them moved. The maintenance nobody schedules is the walk back through the product asking, of each thing, is this where the hand would look for it. Usually the answer is no, and usually the fix is just to move it.

The lesson under the pricing change is harder and more honest. Taking a benefit away is a real cost, and the temptation is always to dress it up as something other than what it is. It was not dressed up. Ad-free was retired, plainly, on the reasoning that the ad surface should be the same for everyone and that the exemption was worth more as reach than as a perk. Whether that trade is right will show in the numbers over the next stretch, not in the confidence of the decision today. But the discipline that matters is the one applied afterward, the pass through every doc and test and tier page to make sure none of them still quietly promises the thing that no longer exists. A policy change that lives only in the code and not in the words around the code is a policy change that will come back as a broken promise later. Today the words got moved to match the machine.

## What's Next

The restructure is the kind of thing that reveals its own next steps once it lands, because moving the tools closer changes what people reach for, and what people reach for tells you what to build. The pricing change starts a clock, and the only useful response to it is to watch honestly and be willing to reverse it if the reach is not worth the friction it buys. The Round still waits behind its flag for the walk that decides it. But the shape of the day was clear and worth keeping: shorten the distance between the tool and the hand, and when you change the deal, change the words too, so the product never says one thing while it does another. More of that next. Find the tool that drifted, and walk it home.
