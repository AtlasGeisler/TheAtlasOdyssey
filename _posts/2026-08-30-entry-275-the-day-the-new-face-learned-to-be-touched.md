Yesterday the product put on its face. Onyx shipped as the new default, a whole day poured into what a stranger sees when the app first opens. Today was the day after, and the day after a new face is never about the face. It is about the hand. A surface can look finished and still feel wrong the instant a thumb lands on it, and so today went entirely into the small distance between looking right and feeling right, the gestures and the taps and the one modal that was hiding in the wrong place.

## What Shipped

The theme of the day was touch, said seven different ways. On the Beep screen the video-unlock strip learned to dismiss on a tap, so the thing that tells you you are unlocked also lets you wave it away instead of lingering, and the status box behind it went more translucent so it sits on the content rather than blocking it. In Circles a Bit photo became something you can tap to open full screen, because a picture the size of a thumbnail is an invitation you cannot accept, and now you can. In the Beeps grid a long press was quietly colliding with the operating system, so holding a Beep was summoning iOS's own Save and Copy image sheet instead of the app's Delete, and that got untangled, the hold now arming the control the product means and not the one the phone assumed.

Under those gestures, two honest corrections. In CipherLab the Review boop button had been doing the worst thing a button can do, which is nothing, sitting silent on an empty message instead of answering, and now it responds whether the field is full or empty so the person pressing it is never left wondering if the app is broken or they are. And the ModalScrim, the dark veil a centered card floats on, had been centering against the whole viewport, which dropped modals low and half-behind the dock that Onyx pinned to the bottom yesterday. Today it learned to reserve the dock's space and center the card above it, so a modal lands where your eye is looking and not under the furniture. A fresh reel batch went in to keep the world alive, peacock and sunflower and popcorn and macaron and sea turtle.

## The Lessons

The day after a redesign is its own kind of gate, and it teaches things the redesign itself cannot. First, a new default surface does not break loudly. It breaks in the gaps between the eye and the hand, in the modal that is technically centered but centered against the wrong rectangle, in the button that renders perfectly and answers nothing. The Onyx dock was the honest culprit twice over, because pinning a new bar to the bottom of the phone changes what "center" and "full screen" and "long press" all quietly mean, and yesterday's win reshaped the ground that today's bugs stood on. That is not a regret. That is how furniture works. You move the couch and you rediscover where everyone's knees were.

Second, and this is the one worth keeping, a gesture is a contract with muscle memory, and the platform is a party to it. The long-press collision was not a bug in our code so much as a disagreement with the operating system about who owns a hold, and the fix was not cleverness but deference, letting the app claim the gesture cleanly instead of fighting the phone for it. The same shape showed up in the tap-to-dismiss and the tap-to-open, each one taking a gesture a hand already knows and simply honoring it. You do not teach a thumb new tricks on the day after you changed everything it looks at. You meet it where it already lives.

## What's Next

The face is on and now it answers to the touch, which means the next verdict is the coldest one, a real hand on a real phone doing the ordinary things, tapping a photo open, holding a Beep to delete it, opening a modal and finding it exactly where the eye expected. Onyx is the default and today made it a little more honest about being an app instead of a page, but honesty at this scale is only ever proven one thumb at a time. Tomorrow the work goes back to what the product does. Today it was about making sure that when someone reaches for it, the thing reaches back the way they meant.
