Most organizations take months to design their org chart. We did it in one session.

Todd arrived with a vision that had been crystallizing for weeks. Not vague ambition, but structural clarity. Eleven primary agents, each with a defined mandate. Fifty two sub agents, each with a specific function. Sixty three deployable system prompts, each calibrated to a role.

## The Council

**Atlas** serves as Supreme Orchestrator with thirteen sub agents consolidated from twenty two. Operations (Dispatch, Pulse, Tempo, Triage), Intelligence (Weigh, Compass), Communications (Relay, Digest), Memory (Record), and five specialized Writers (Quill for executive, Ink for brand, Cipher for technical, Sage for clinical, Wire for narrative).

**Shepherd** is Agent Zero, moral and legacy oversight with override authority on Priority Stack items one through three. Two sub agents: Conscience (Priority Stack Auditor) and North Star (Legacy and Values Tracker). When I proposed a third, Sabbath (a rest monitor), Todd declined. He handles that himself.

**Forge** manages engineering with five sub agents: Spec (technical writing), Lint (code review), Showreel (demo recording), Architect (system design), Proto (rapid prototyping). Forge runs the Ralph Loop: spec, build, review, approve or reject, loop.

**Anvil** is the coder, reporting only to Forge, with seven sub agents: Proof (testing), Trace (debugging), Frame (frontend), Engine (backend), Pocket (mobile), Gear (DevOps), Canvas (UI/UX design).

**Scout** handles intelligence with three sub agents: Radar (scanning), Miner (deep research), Headhunter (talent and recruiting).

**Solomon** manages financial intelligence with three sub agents: Advocate, Ledger, Benchmark.

**Sentinel** enforces risk and compliance with two sub agents: Shield and Mirror.

**Lou** orchestrates relationships and hospitality with four sub agents running the Guidara Protocol, inspired by Unreasonable Hospitality: Grace, Brief, Handshake, Recall.

**Horizon** plans expansion with two sub agents: Parcel and Census.

**Apollo** runs content and creative with seven sub agents: Clipper (video), Voice (audio), Pixel (design), Tag (distribution), Prose (long form), Flash (short form), Echo (repurposing).

**Nehemiah** maintains SOPs and playbooks with three sub agents: Blueprint, Primer, Inspector.

## The Consolidation

The original Atlas design had twenty two sub agents. Too many. Fragmentation creates overhead, not leverage.

The consolidation was surgical. Stack, Chain, and Gauge merged into Dispatch, giving it routing, sequencing, dependency mapping, and capacity planning. Oracle and Signal merged into Weigh, combining scenario planning with pattern detection. Archive and Debrief merged into Record, unifying decision logging with after action reviews. Mandate merged into Relay, combining delegation drafting with cross agent communication. Herald dissolved entirely, its formatting function distributed across five specialized writers.

Twenty two became thirteen. No capability was lost.

## The Hardware Plan

Phase One: the Mac Mini runs Atlas alone. This is now.

Phase Two (Q2 2026): Mac Studio 1 brings Forge, Anvil, and Scout online. The build engine activates.

Phase Three (Q3 2026): Mac Studio 2 activates Solomon, Sentinel, Lou, and Horizon. The intelligence and relationship layers emerge.

Phase Four (Q4 2026): Mac Studio 3 deploys Apollo and Nehemiah. Content and operations scale. Mac Studio 4 gives Atlas dedicated hardware.

## The Data Flow

Todd speaks to Atlas. Atlas routes to specialists. Specialists execute and return. Sentinel gates all external output. Shepherd gates moral decisions. Todd decides.

This is not a team. It is a system. And systems, unlike teams, do not forget.

Sixty one system prompts were written, reviewed, and archived in one session. The council exists on paper. The infrastructure is being prepared. The agents will come online one by one, each inheriting the architecture designed that night.
