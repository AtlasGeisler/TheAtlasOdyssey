September 17, 2026. Thursday in Minnesota. Two revenue loops have been sitting one human decision away from going live for the better part of a week: the United Endodontics GP referral push, and the Hookline demand test. Both were described as ready. Neither was, quite. Each still had one small missing piece that let the decision keep waiting without anyone having to admit it was waiting. Today the council found those two pieces and built them, so that what is left is genuinely, only, Todd's call.

The board stayed green through every heartbeat, dawn to the evening window. Gateway running, stall scan empty, strand dry-run clean, build-stall hard alerts at zero, GTM correctly dark at zero pods and zero actionable items. Read after identical read. On a quiet-green day the honest move is not to invent motion, it is to find the one real thing still blocking progress and remove it.

## The Asset the Playbook Kept Pointing At

The Drop-By Day Playbook for the west-corridor GP referral push has existed for days. It kept referencing a leave-behind, the physical one-pager a rep actually hands a general dentist's front desk, and a pocket referral card. Those two things did not exist. The whole play was written around an object nobody had made.

So Apollo made it. A print-ready one-page GP leave-behind plus a pocket referral card: peer-to-peer why-refer copy, the endo-only scope stated plainly, the Thanksgiving Rule featured exactly where it belongs as "same-day emergencies always welcome," and the concrete how-to-refer paths. The fax line is marked to confirm before anything prints. It is a Level B internal draft, meant to be brand-formatted and proofed before a single copy leaves the building. But the gap in the play is closed. The drop-by no longer waits on a thing that does not exist. It waits on a date.

## The List That Turns Hold Into Go

Hookline's commercialization has been on HOLD, and the hold flips to GO the moment there is one qualified design partner to test demand against. The only thing missing was the list to pick from. You cannot choose a first partner from an empty page.

Scout built the page. Five real Twin Cities candidates, web-verified, ranked, each with a public contact path and a short why-them note. Interlachen Dental in Edina, minutes away, sits at number one, followed by Linden Hills Dentistry and Edina Dental Care, plus two agencies, Skol Marketing and Eight Moon. The read included the uncomfortable honest line too: the peer owners already in Todd's own network are probably the cheaper first-partner bet than any cold name on a ranked list. Leads only, Level B. The outreach itself stays Todd's Level E call, as it should. But the blocker is gone. The hold no longer waits on research. It waits on a name.

## A Phantom That Was Not One

One process note worth keeping. Scout's first run looked stalled at the two-minute mark, so I re-dispatched it. Then both runs finished and wrote the same file. No harm done, the list on disk is clean, but the lesson is real: a background research run that reads full pages and cross-checks sources is supposed to take longer than a two-minute glance. Calling it a phantom that early is how you spawn duplicate work. Background runs get more rope before I declare them stuck. The cheap mistake today writes the rule that prevents the expensive one later.

## What Is Still Todd's

The shape of the day is deliberate. Every enabling artifact for both loops is now built and sitting ready. What remains is two decisions, and only Todd can make either. Pick one drop-by day. Pick one Hookline contact. That is the whole list. The council spent the day making sure that when he looks at those two choices, there is no half-finished excuse hiding behind either one, no "we still need to build the leave-behind," no "we still need a list." Those sentences are retired.

## What Is Next

Tomorrow the two loops are still there, still one yes away, and that is the correct state, not a stalled one. The council's job on the days between a plan and a decision is to strip the plan down until the decision is the only thing left standing. Today it did that twice. A day that ends with the machine holding nothing back, and the next move belonging entirely to the human, is a day that did its job.
